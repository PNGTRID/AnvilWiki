The direct answer: a young site monetizes through three paths, and the order matters more than the choice. Affiliate recommendations open first because they need honesty rather than traffic thresholds; display advertising opens second when your content and visits clear an ad network bar; your own product comes last, once you know what readers keep asking for. Trying to run them in the wrong order is how new sites burn trust for pocket change.

## When is a site ready to earn money?

Three signals, roughly in this order:

1. **Search engines send steady visitors** — not viral spikes, a baseline that returns without you pushing.
2. **The content library is substantial** — dozens of complete pages, each genuinely answering something.
3. **Some pages keep pulling readers back** — updated references, checklists, or anything people bookmark.

A site with five pages and no traffic is not "early" in monetization — it is pre-monetization. The work at that stage is the [content workflow](/guides/content-workflow-tutorial/), not revenue experiments.

## Which revenue paths fit a brand-new site?

| Path | What it needs | Fits when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affiliate recommendations | Trust: you actually used the thing and say so honestly | First — from the first weeks of real content |
| Display advertising | An approved application + steady traffic | Second — after the library and visits are real |
| Your own product | A repeated reader question worth solving | Last — once you can see what readers ask for |

<Callout type="tip" title="Why affiliate comes first">An honest affiliate link placed inside a page that already answers the reader costs nothing, has no approval gate, and converts on trust you already earned. It is the only revenue path a one-month-old site can start on legitimately.</Callout>

One exception worth memorizing: **game wikis flip this order** — display advertising comes first there, because game and gear affiliate commissions run thin. This page's affiliate-first order is for general content sites (tools, software, tutorial libraries).

## How does display advertising actually work?

The mechanism, honestly: you apply to an ad network, it checks that your site has real content, real traffic and clean practices, and once approved it fills reserved slots on your pages with paid ads. Your income follows visits and topic — some topics pay fractions of a cent per view, others many times that.

What matters for a young site:

- **Apply when you qualify.** Networks publish their current requirements; meeting them is the gate. Applying earlier with a thin site wastes the attempt.
- **Never manufacture content to pass the gate.** Padding the site with low-effort pages to reach a threshold produces exactly the thin content the review exists to filter — and the penalty outlasts the approval.
- **Few slots, well placed.** One slot where readers naturally rest beats five that interrupt.

## What do affiliate links need to work?

Three ingredients, none optional:

1. **Actual use.** You installed it, read it, played it. Recommendations of things you never used are the fastest route to a site nobody believes.
2. **Clear disclosure.** A short, visible line such as a sponsored label near the link. Disclosure is required by ad rules in many markets and, more importantly, it is what keeps trust intact.
3. **Natural placement.** The link belongs inside the paragraph that solves the related problem — not stacked at the end of every page.

<Callout type="warn" title="The trust red line">One fake or exaggerated recommendation can undo a year of honest content. If you cannot say what a tool is bad at, you do not know it well enough to recommend it.</Callout>

## How do you measure whether any of it is working?

Three dashboards, checked weekly, nothing more:

- **A traffic panel** — which pages bring visitors, from where, on what devices.
- **A search panel** — which queries show your pages and where you rank; this is how you find pages that are close to winning and deserve an improvement pass.
- **A revenue panel** — clicks, conversions, payouts, per path.

The discipline that matters: **decisions follow the numbers on the panels, not the feeling of the week.** If a page ranks ninth for a decent query, improving it beats writing a new page; the panel told you that.

## What should the first month look like?

A realistic first month of a monetization-minded site:

| Week | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Ship the first pages with the content workflow; place nothing yet |
| 2 | Keep shipping; add your first honest affiliate link where it truly helps |
| 3 | Install the traffic and search panels; learn what the baseline looks like |
| 4 | Review the numbers, pick the next ten pages from what they say |

Advertising enters the plan when the library and the traffic meet a network bar — usually month three or later for a diligent solo site.
