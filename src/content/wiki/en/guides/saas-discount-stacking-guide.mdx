Most people overpay for software not because they miss a coupon, but because they apply their discounts in the wrong order — or miss that two discounts they already qualify for were never combined. Stacking is a small, legal discipline: know the layers, apply them in order, and read the one paragraph of terms that actually matters. This guide uses our two covered tools, a design app and a notes app, as worked examples.

## What does stacking a SaaS discount actually mean?

Three reductions can apply to the same invoice, and they come from different places:

1. **Billing cycle** — paying yearly instead of monthly is a built-in discount, typically 15-25%, that needs no code at all.
2. **Coupon codes** — the promotional layer: seasonal codes, newsletter codes, partner bundles. This is the layer our codes pages track.
3. **Program pricing** — student, nonprofit, education, and startup programs that discount (or grant) the plan itself.

Stacking means combining two or three of these layers on one subscription. The skill is knowing which layers a vendor lets combine — and applying them in the order that produces the lowest invoice.

## The three layers and the order they apply in

Apply discounts in this fixed order and the math takes care of itself:

1. **Choose the billing cycle first.** This sets the base price the rest is calculated against. Yearly billing lowers the base; a coupon applied afterwards compounds against the already-lowered number.
2. **Redeem the coupon second.** Paste the code in the billing panel and watch the invoice preview recalculate before confirming.
3. **Apply program pricing last.** If you qualify for a program rate, check it against the stacked total rather than assuming it wins — sometimes program pricing replaces the coupon entirely (see below).

One ordering trap: some vendors calculate coupons against the list price regardless of cycle, which makes monthly-plus-coupon occasionally cheaper than yearly-plus-coupon for short holding periods. Always compare the invoice preview, not the advertised percentages.

## What usually cannot be stacked

Three combinations are rejected or forbidden almost everywhere:

1. **Two coupons on one invoice.** Checkout accepts one promo code; a "combine with another code" trick does not exist in any mainstream billing system.
2. **Program pricing plus a seasonal coupon.** Student, nonprofit, and startup rates usually replace promotional codes. Compare totals instead of assuming they merge.
3. **Trial plus coupon on the same account.** Most vendors require the trial to end (or be cancelled) before a code applies. Do not create a second account to force the combination — that is the clause terms call abuse, and it forfeits both discounts.

## The terms worth actually reading

You do not need the full legal text. Three sentences decide whether a stack is legitimate:

- **One redemption per account** — the sentence that defines how many times a code works for you. Respect it; account cycling is the fastest way to lose the account.
- **Discounts apply from the next billing period** — the sentence that explains why a code redeemed today shows up on next month's invoice, not this one.
- **Promotional codes cannot be combined** — the sentence that overrides any stacking you read online, when present. If it is absent, the allowed combinations above are fair game.

Stacking through the normal checkout flow — the right cycle, one code, honest program eligibility — is not a loophole.
