Picking the niche is the highest-leverage decision in this whole series: a mediocre site on the right topic outearns a beautiful site on the wrong one. The direct answer to "how do I pick": collect candidates from real community demand, check whether existing search results actually answer the searcher, score what survives, and commit within two days. This tutorial walks each step; the next one, [how to build the site with AI](/guides/build-site-with-ai-tutorial/), turns the verdict into a live website.

## Why does the niche decide more than the site itself?

A website is a container; the niche is what fills it. The niche determines three things you cannot fix later without starting over: what people search for, how hard the existing results are to beat, and whether the topic can carry monetization at all. Rebranding a site costs an afternoon. Re-theming it to a different topic costs every page you wrote.

That is why this tutorial spends a full lesson on choosing, and why the rule is **validate before you build** — not build first and hope demand shows up.

## What makes a niche worth building?

Four conditions, all four, no exceptions:

1. **Real search demand.** People type questions about the topic into a search box — not occasionally, but as a recurring habit.
2. **Beatable results.** Page one is not fully occupied by answers that truly solve the searcher's problem.
3. **Sustainable content.** You can keep producing accurate pages for months: the facts are checkable and the topic has more than three articles in it.
4. **A monetization path.** Advertising, affiliate recommendations, or something you sell — the topic must have at least one realistic way to earn.

<Callout type="tip" title="The fourth condition is the one beginners skip">A topic can pass all three content checks and still be unmotizable. Ask the monetization question while the niche is still a candidate, not after thirty pages exist.</Callout>

## How do you find niche candidates?

Candidates should come from observed demand, not from imagination. Four channels, in rough order of usefulness:

| Channel | What to look for | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search autocomplete | Partial queries that the engine completes for you | Autocomplete reflects what many people actually type |
| Community forums and subreddits | The same question asked repeatedly, answered poorly | Repetition plus weak answers is the strongest opening signal |
| Trend tools | Topics rising over months, not spiking for a day | Rising demand rewards early, complete answers |
| Your own logs and chats | Questions friends or colleagues ask you twice | If a question reaches you twice, it reaches a search box thousands of times |

Collect ten to twenty candidates into a simple table before judging any of them. Judgment comes later; today is collection.

## How do you validate a niche before building?

This is the step most tutorials skip, and it is where this series differs. A full page of search results can still be an open seat — if most results are answering the wrong question. Run the **intent-satisfaction check** on each shortlisted candidate:

1. **Write what the searcher is trying to solve** in one sentence, behind the query words.
2. **Open the actual results** and note what each one really answers.
3. **Count how many answer it right** — completely, currently, honestly.
4. **Two or fewer means build** — one page that truly answers the question beats seven that answer a neighboring one.
5. **Plan to answer in the first paragraph** — that is how your page wins the seat.

A worked example (fictional topic): the query "restore scratched game discs". Nine of ten results discuss selling used discs; one forum thread from years ago actually explains restoration. That is a full-looking page with one real answer — a build.

## How do you score and decide?

Give every surviving candidate a simple 1–5 on five rows and total it:

| Row | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Demand | Are people visibly asking, repeatedly? |
| Competition | Can a thorough, current answer beat what ranks today? |
| Intent gap | Do fewer than two results answer the real question? |
| Content depth | Can you list 15+ accurate articles the topic needs? |
| Monetization | Is there at least one realistic earning path? |

Take the highest total, write one line in a decision log — *build / drop / needs-more-data* — and set a review date. The decision is yours; AI can fill the table, but it should never make the call.

## Common niche-picking mistakes

- **Reading the words, missing the purpose.** High search volume on a generic word often means the results answer something entirely different. Open the results page before trusting any number.
- **Substituting "I can write it" for "people want it."** Ability to produce content is not demand. Watch what searchers are trying to solve.
- **Endless research.** Every extra week of validation is a week the site does not exist. Two days, then decide.
