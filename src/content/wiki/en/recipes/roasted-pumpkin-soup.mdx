## Roasting is the recipe

Boiled pumpkin tastes like wet vegetable. Roasted pumpkin caramelises its natural sugars, and those browned edges are the entire flavour base - the soup needs no cream, no stock cubes, no sugar. Cut wedges, toss with oil, and roast at 200 degrees Celsius for thirty minutes without stirring; the onion and unpeeled garlic go on the same tray and squeeze out of their skins afterwards.

## The method

1. Roast the pumpkin, onion, and garlic as above until the edges darken.
2. Scoop the flesh into a pot, squeeze the garlic out of its skins, add the onion.
3. Pour in hot stock and blend directly in the pot until completely smooth.
4. Season, heat through, and serve - a thread of cream or toasted seeds on top if you like.

## The hot-stock rule

Cold stock shocks the blend and dulls the flavour the same way cold milk dulls coffee. Heat the stock before it meets the pumpkin - it costs nothing and it is the difference between soup that tastes bright and soup that tastes thin.
