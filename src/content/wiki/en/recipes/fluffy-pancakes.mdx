## The lumpy batter rule is not a suggestion

Baking powder starts working the moment it meets liquid. Every extra stir pops bubbles you paid for with the buttermilk. Mix until the flour disappears - ten to fifteen strokes - and walk away for ten minutes. The batter will rise and the surface will pit; that is the leavening doing its job, and it is also why the [vanilla sponge cake](/recipes/classic-vanilla-sponge-cake/) treats air as the whole recipe.

## The method

1. Whisk the dry ingredients in a bowl; whisk buttermilk, egg, and melted butter in a jug.
2. Pour wet into dry, stir briefly, and stop while the batter still looks wrong.
3. Rest ten minutes. No stirring during the rest, no matter how lumpy it looks.
4. Ladle onto medium-low heat, dry pan. Flip when the edges matte and bubbles pop on top - about two minutes a side.
5. Hold finished pancakes on a rack, never stacked on a plate - trapped steam softens the rise you worked for.

## Topping strategy

Butter and real maple syrup need nothing else. Fruit compotes and whipped cream turn breakfast into dessert, which is legitimate - and if breakfast dessert is the goal, yesterday's [chocolate chip cookies](/recipes/chocolate-chip-cookies/) crumbled over the stack is the house speciality. For a savoury counterweight later in the day, the [tomato garlic pasta](/recipes/tomato-garlic-pasta/) shares this recipe's one-bowl spirit.
