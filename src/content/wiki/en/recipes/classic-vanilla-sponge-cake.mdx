## What makes a vanilla sponge rise without baking powder?

Mechanical leavening. Whipped whole eggs hold more air than any chemical raising agent, and that foam is the only thing lifting this crumb. Three conditions keep it stable: eggs at room temperature (cold yolks resist whipping), sugar added gradually so the foam keeps its gloss, and flour folded - never stirred - so the bubbles survive the trip to the oven.

## The method, step by step

1. Heat the oven to 180 degrees Celsius and line the tin base - no greasing the sides, the batter needs grip to climb.
2. Whisk eggs and sugar over gentle heat until the mixture reaches the ribbon stage: the trail of batter sitting on the surface should hold for three seconds.
3. Sift a third of the flour over the foam and fold with a spatula, cutting through the middle and rotating the bowl. Repeat in two more additions.
4. Stream in the melted butter and vanilla down the spatula, folding just until the streaks disappear.
5. Bake 28 to 30 minutes. The sponge is done when the top springs back and a skewer comes out clean.

## Two variations worth knowing

Swap the vanilla for lemon zest and you have the base of most European coffee-club sponges; fold in sifted cocoa with the flour for a chocolate version. ## Serving and storage

Cool fully in the tin, then wrap. The crumb stays soft for two days at room temperature and freezes well for a month - slice before freezing so you can pull single portions.
