## Why brown the butter?

Melted butter alone makes a fine cookie. Browned butter makes a memorable one: the milk solids toast while the water cooks off, leaving a nutty base that reads as toffee against the chocolate. It costs five extra minutes and no extra ingredients - the same trade the [vanilla sponge cake](/recipes/classic-vanilla-sponge-cake/) makes by whipping eggs properly.

## The method

1. Brown the butter in a light-coloured pan until the milk solids turn hazelnut-brown. Cool until solid but pliable.
2. Cream with both sugars for two minutes, then beat in the egg, yolk, and vanilla.
3. Add flour and baking soda, then fold in the chopped chocolate - chopped bars give puddles and shards; chips give uniform dots.
4. Scoop sixteen portions, chill thirty minutes.
5. Bake at 180 degrees Celsius for ten to eleven minutes. Pull them when the edges set but the centres still look underdone - they finish on the sheet.

## Portioning is the whole game

An ice cream scoop keeps sixteen cookies identical, which means they bake identically. Freehand spoonfuls give you some raw middles and some biscuit-shooters on the same tray.
