## Walk one complete example first (fictional game Starvale)

Say your candidate table turns up the query `starvale nostalgia`. One search, and page one is packed — by Lesson 5's standard, a "red ocean". Don't leave yet. **Ask one question, row by row: is this result answering "nostalgia"?**

| Page-one entry | What it actually answers | Right answer? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Official wiki: full mechanics reference | Gameplay lookup | ❌ A different question |
| 2. Forum thread: 2019 screenshots | Show-and-tell chat, scattered | ⚠️ Adjacent, not an answer |
| 3. A 2019 nostalgia essay | Nostalgia — but seven years stale | ⚠️ Right question, outdated answer |
| 4. A playthrough video | Watching someone play, not looking up nostalgia | ❌ |
| 5-8. Guide sites: boss guides / codes / tier list | Their own keywords | ❌ All answering other questions |

Count them: fewer than **2 of 10 truly answer "nostalgia"**, and the best one is outdated. You don't need to beat ten sites — you need one page that **truly answers nostalgia**: how the feel shifted between which versions, which removed mechanics players still mourn, where a returning player should pick the game back up. That one page shoves aside the seven rivals that answered the wrong question.

This move is an **intent-satisfaction check**: page one can be full while the seats for right answers sit empty.

## The five-step checklist (take it)

1. **What is the player trying to solve with this query?** (nostalgia? codes? a build decision? — one sentence)
2. **What does each page-one result actually answer?** (row by row, like the table above)
3. **How many answer it right?**
4. **Two or fewer → build**, newer, fuller, and more complete than they are
5. **Answer straight in the first paragraph** — the player's question is the first sentence's job; no warm-up

## Three classic mistakes (made for you in advance)

- **Reading the words, missing the purpose**: "nostalgia" is about feeling, not mechanics. Watch what the player wants, not what the query looks like.
- **Substituting "I can write it" for "the player wants it"**: being able to write a boss guide doesn't mean this searcher wants one. Read the question before answering it.
- **Right answer, buried**: a history lesson in paragraph one, the answer in paragraph five — the player bounces after eight seconds, and Google records the dissatisfaction.

## Three words to know (just these)

- **Search intent**: what the player is really trying to solve behind the query. The words are the shell; the intent is the question.
- **Intent satisfaction**: how much of page one actually answers the question — it decides whether you have a seat.
- **Direct answer**: the player's answer in the first sentence. The first discipline of every writing lesson in this manual.

## ✅ Acceptance (all must hold)

- You ran one query through all five steps and got a build / don't-build plus a how
- You can say how many page-one results truly answered, and where you'll be better
- ☐ You remember: full ≠ no chance; answering right = the chance
