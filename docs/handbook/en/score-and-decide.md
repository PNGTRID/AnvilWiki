## A true scene first

Ten rows, every one carrying trend and competition labels. Where beginners lose the most here is not choosing wrong — it is **choosing forever**. Two weeks of comparing, and the golden window closes. This lesson hands you two tables and one discipline: selection races the clock; it is not research.

## Step 1: the scorecard (5 minutes per candidate)

Four items, 100 points, **60 and above goes to decision**:

| Item | Points | How to judge |
|---|---|---|
| Enough to write about (hard gate) | 25 | Enough mechanics, items, bosses for guides? Not enough — score 0 no matter the rest |
| Popularity evidence | 25 | Comments, subs, concurrents; dev still updating = window still open |
| Will players search | 25 | Do they type "game + wiki / codes"? (Lesson 4's label) |
| English content gap | 25 | Thin English guides = popularity evidence and your open seat at once |

## Step 2: the decision ledger (your selection asset)

Open the table that follows you for the life of the project:

```
Game | Found date | Trend | Competition | Score | Decision (build/drop/consider) | Decision date | Site link
```

Three usage rules:

- **"Build" rows**: fill the decision date; every later lesson's output hangs on this row.
- **"Drop" rows**: write one line of why (crowded / fading / can't write it). Six months on, these reasons are your most valuable experience.
- **"Consider" rows**: not a graveyard — a schedule. **Recheck the trend the next day**; two weeks without a rise, flip to "drop". Never let "consider" pile up unbounded.

## Step 3: the two iron rules

1. **60+ points AND competition "open/beatable" = start now.** There is no "compare a bit more" option.
2. **From starting selection to decision: 2 days maximum.** When time's up, take the highest score on the table — the biggest cost of selection is time.

## Let AI run the whole funnel (recommended)

Feed the real material to AI (ZCode / Claude Code / Codex, or web ChatGPT), **replace every `<>` and paste it whole**:

```text
You are a game wiki selection analyst. Evaluate this candidate through a four-layer funnel: discovery → scoring → demand validation → competition validation.
[Material]
Game: <game name>
Google Trends: <paste or describe "{game} codes/wiki" vs the baseline query>
Google page one: <paste "{game} wiki" and "{game} codes" result lists, one line each>
Other signals: <YouTube views / Roblox concurrents / community buzz — whatever you have>
[Tasks]
1. Scorecard (0-100, ≥60 passes): content depth (hard gate) / social proof / title search-friendliness / English content gap
2. Demand validation: read the three things (level / slope / cycle); does the two-source rule hold
3. Competition: judge each page-one result open / beatable / drop, and name the gatekeeper's weakness
4. Intent satisfaction: how many results truly answer the player's question
5. Verdict: build / consider / drop / watch a week, with reasons in three lines or fewer
Analysis only — write no files. List anything missing and ask me.
```

Note the last line: during selection AI advises only; it never touches your repo. The person deciding is you.

## Three classic mistakes (made for you in advance)

- **Endless comparing**: two candidates both at 60 — take the one with the emptier results page and **start today**.
- **Oscillating at 59 vs 61**: the default move is always "drop, next row". The pool has more.
- **Selecting without recording**: a choice that never enters the ledger never happened. This table is the base document for your later retrospectives and your second site.

## Three words to know (just these)

- **Hard gate**: enough to write about. Fail this and the score is meaningless.
- **Decision ledger**: your selection register — every candidate's book of life and death.
- **Commit**: a build/drop decision with a date on it. Undated decisions don't count.

## ✅ Acceptance (all must hold)

- At least one candidate row reads "build", with its score and two pieces of popularity evidence
- The decision ledger exists, and every "drop" row has a reason
- ☐ Start to decision took no more than 2 days
