## A true scene first

Two beginners start the same day. A thinks of a game and builds immediately — two weeks later the site is done and almost nobody searches the game. B spends three days filling a 12-row candidate table, validates row by row, and picks a rising new game at row five. Three weeks later A is writing the eighth article for a site nobody visits; B's site is already being indexed.

The difference is not diligence. It is the **candidate pool**. Judging one game takes 10 minutes; what actually decides your output is how many options you hold. So lesson one of picking is not judging — it is **collecting**.

## Step 1: scout three channels daily (20 minutes)

| Where | What to watch | Pick |
|---|---|---|
| [itch.io newest](https://itch.io/games/newest) (filter: Play in browser) | New games daily, fewest watchers, least competition | 3-5 |
| [Steam New & Trending / wishlist charts](https://store.steampowered.com/explore/new/) | High quality but crowded with scouts — move fast | 3-5 |
| [Roblox Discover](https://www.roblox.com/discover) (sort: Rising / Up-and-Coming) | Where "redeem codes" searchers are densest | 3-5 |

One picking standard only: **does the game have enough mechanics, items, and bosses to write guides about**? Pure match-three games have no guide demand — skip. Unsure? Log it anyway; the next lesson verifies with data.

## Step 2: log the candidate table

Open a table (paper or spreadsheet — it becomes the workbench for every later lesson):

```
Game | Channel | Date found | One line: why worth a look
```

**The date is mandatory** — later lessons keep asking "how long has this game been out"; without dates you cannot answer.

Rhythm: 20 minutes daily, a steady intake of 3-5 rows. **Do not build anything until the table holds 10 rows.** A thin pool turns "validation" into "talking yourself into it".

## Three classic mistakes (made for you in advance)

- **Logging only what you like.** Your taste is not search demand. This lesson only collects; "do people search it" belongs to the next lesson's data.
- **One candidate and go.** A single option means no comparison — you cannot know the one you hold is the better one. Ten rows minimum.
- **Scouting one day only.** The pool is a pipeline, not a one-time purchase. Twenty minutes daily keeps selection supplied forever.

## Three words to know (just these)

- **Candidate pool**: every game awaiting validation — your selection inventory.
- **Channel**: a place new games surface. The three above are starters; they last a long time.
- **Golden window**: the 2-8 weeks after a game takes off, holding the bulk of its lifetime searches — the date you logged is how you judge the window.

- Each row answers "why logged" in one sentence
- ☐ The 20-minute scout is now a daily habit
