## A true scene first

Two games in the candidate table. The first: high YouTube view counts. The second: modest views, but comments full of "any new codes?". Which one?

Probably the second — but don't guess; verify with data. This lesson's job: stamp every row of the candidate table with a rising / flat / falling label.

## Step 1: open compare mode (1 minute)

Go to [trends.google.com](https://trends.google.com), hit **Compare**, enter:

- `your game codes`
- a baseline query whose scale you roughly know (no idea? use `roblox codes` — a perennial giant)

## Step 2: read exactly three things (2 minutes)

| What | How to judge |
|---|---|
| **Level** | Your line at what fraction of the baseline? Half or more = a big query; hugging the floor = niche — drop it or watch it longer |
| **Slope** | Rising over the last 90 days = the golden window may be open now; flat = an old game with a settled competitive board; falling = fading, don't catch the knife |
| **Cycle** | Swings with seasons/patches are a normal shape; a long slide with no bounce = demand is leaving |

Best practice: screenshot the curve, feed it to AI with the three questions, and let it read the chart against the table above — but you place the label. The judgment is yours, not its.

## Step 3: the two-source rule (2 minutes)

Trends says rising — still **not enough**. Verify once more in a second, unrelated place: a relevant YouTube video passing 50k views in 7 days, or Roblox concurrent players.

**Two independent sources both hot → next lesson.** Only one source hot → mark the row "watch", and look again in a week. The most common single-source mirage: huge YouTube views, flat Trends — viewers watch and leave; they never search for guides, and that traffic can never become your visits.

## Three classic mistakes (made for you in advance)

- **Slope without level**: 0.1% → 0.3% is "rising", but that scale cannot hold up a site. Level too low means "watch", however steep the climb.
- **Deciding on one source**: trusting Trends alone is this lesson's most expensive mistake. The two-source rule has no exceptions.
- **Verifying an English market with Chinese-language heat**: your site is in English; validate the words English players type (`game codes`), not the buzz in your own feed.

## Three words to know (just these)

- **Baseline query**: a query whose scale you know, used as the ruler for your candidate.
- **Two-source rule**: popularity evidence from two unrelated places, so one platform's fake heat can't fool you.
- **Watch**: the middle state in your table — not rejected, just "look again in a week".

## ✅ Acceptance (all must hold)

- Every candidate row carries a rising / flat / falling label with its evidence
- Every "rising" has two independent sources behind it
- ☐ You skipped no verification because something "felt hot"
