## A true scene first

"Page one is full" is where beginners give up most often — one search, ten results, walk away. But those ten results are **not the same species**: a forum thread dead for two years and a daily-updated pro wiki are two completely different opponents. This lesson reads page one down to one sentence: open seats, beatable, or drop.

## Step 1: search both queries (30 seconds)

Google `game wiki` and `game codes`, read page one of each. Both — the wiki query and the codes query often have entirely different competition, and the codes query frequently has open seats.

## Step 2: match what you see (1 minute)

| You see | It means | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Under 10 results, or mostly PDFs, forum threads, YouTube videos, social posts | No serious site works this query | **Open seats — build now** |
| A [Fandom](https://www.fandom.com) page that's thin, missing entries, untouched for months | Demand exists; the gatekeeper is slacking | **Beatable**: take the spot with fuller, fresher content |
| [Fandom](https://www.fandom.com) or [Game8](https://game8.co), thick and updated daily | A professional team runs it; you can't out-slog them | **Drop — next candidate** |

## Step 3: write the verdict back into the table

Next to the trend label, add a competition column: open / beatable / drop. Every candidate now has two labels — but hold the decision. **A full page one ≠ no chance**; one last, most-missed signal gets its own lesson next.

## Three classic mistakes (made for you in advance)

- **Counting results, not weighing them**: ten forum threads and ten pro sites are different worlds. Read "who is the opponent", not "how many".
- **Folding at the sight of Fandom**: Fandom is a player-built wiki platform, and a huge share of its pages are abandoned at birth. The thin, stale one is the easiest to beat — players want complete and current, and you can deliver both.
- **Checking one query only**: skipping `codes` misses open seats constantly. Two queries, each read.

## Three words to know (just these)

- **SERP**: Search Engine Results Page — Google's results page. The first ten slots are the golden shelf; most clicks land there.
- **Open seats**: search demand no result on page one truly satisfies — a new site's biggest opportunity.
- **Hard veto**: pro sites thick and daily-updated kill the candidate no matter its other scores. Competition is the only veto.

## ✅ Acceptance (all must hold)

- Every candidate checked both queries, competition column filled: open / beatable / drop
- For every "beatable" you can name the gatekeeper's weakness (thin where, stale where)
- ☐ You never lingered on a candidate marked "drop"
